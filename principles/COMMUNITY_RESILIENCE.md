# Keep It Local — Principle: Community Resilience

## What it means

Community resilience begins with knowing what a community depends on:

- electricity;
- water;
- telecommunications and internet;
- cloud services and data centers;
- transportation;
- emergency services;
- digital government;
- financial services;
- and critical suppliers.

A resilient community can still use cloud services, external AI, public networks, regional utilities, and commercial platforms. It should, however, know **what depends on them, what happens when they fail, what alternatives exist, and what recovery looks like**.

## Why it matters

Communities are increasingly surrounded by large digital infrastructure while many of the people living in them have little understanding of how dependent their own lives and businesses have become on distant computing infrastructure.

The framework approaches this from the other direction:

> **Before asking how much more infrastructure we need, let's understand what we already depend on — and what we can control ourselves.**

## What it shapes in the framework

- The [Dependency Model](../models/dependency-model.md) provides the vocabulary for community dependency mapping.
- The [Infrastructure Stack](../models/infrastructure-stack.md) connects digital dependencies to physical layers — power, cooling, water, and supply chains.
- The [Resilience Model](../models/resilience-model.md) guides how a community analyzes outages and recovery.
- The [Community Infrastructure](../community-infrastructure/README.md) application layer applies all of this to a community context.

## Core understanding

> **Local resilience begins with knowing what you have the ability to control.**
