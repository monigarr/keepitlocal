# EO_14420_14421 — Bulk-Power Security, AI Infrastructure & Keep It Local

## Purpose

This folder documents the relationship between the August 26, 2026 U.S. presidential action declaring a national emergency concerning the U.S. bulk-power system and the **Keep It Local** project.

The project is not anti-AI. It is concerned with **infrastructure literacy, community resilience, data sovereignty, and the ability of communities to understand the dependencies created by large-scale digital infrastructure**.

The Executive Order explicitly identifies rapid growth in advanced manufacturing, data centers, artificial intelligence, and defense production as factors increasing dependence on reliable electricity and magnifying the consequences of grid disruption.

## Core thesis

> AI infrastructure is physical infrastructure.

A responsible assessment of AI/data-center development therefore needs to examine more than buildings, servers, jobs, tax revenue, and electricity consumption. It should also examine the transmission system, substations, transformers, control systems, software, firmware, remote access, maintenance/update mechanisms, supply chains, resilience, and who bears the consequences of failure.

## Why this matters to Akwesasne

The proposed large-scale data-center development near Massena is geographically close to Akwesasne. The Mohawk Council of Akwesasne has publicly raised concerns about energy and water demand, local power-system strain, environmental impacts, health and safety, consultation, and protection of Akwesasne lands and waters.

This repository does **not** claim that the Executive Order gives Akwesasne authority over the Massena project, nor that it automatically applies to Canadian portions of Akwesasne. Instead, it identifies a new federal critical-infrastructure lens that can complement community, environmental, regulatory, and Indigenous governance concerns.

## Important research discipline

This repository separates:

1. **Documented facts**
2. **Policy interpretation**
3. **Technical inference**
4. **Questions requiring evidence**

It should not be used to assert that a particular vendor, country, piece of equipment, facility, or project is a national-security threat unless competent authorities or primary evidence establish that fact.
