# AI / Data-Center Critical Infrastructure Checklist

## Purpose

Use this checklist when evaluating a large AI, hyperscale, or other high-load digital facility.

The purpose is **infrastructure transparency and resilience**, not opposition to technology.

---

## A. Electrical Load

- [ ] What is the requested electrical load in MW?
- [ ] What is the expected continuous load?
- [ ] What is the maximum peak load?
- [ ] Is the load phased?
- [ ] What assumptions support the load forecast?
- [ ] Who is responsible for verifying those assumptions?

## B. Interconnection

- [ ] Which utility provides service?
- [ ] Which transmission lines supply the project?
- [ ] What voltage levels are involved?
- [ ] Which substations are involved?
- [ ] Are new substations required?
- [ ] Are existing substations being expanded?
- [ ] Are transmission upgrades required?
- [ ] Who pays for the upgrades?
- [ ] Who owns the resulting infrastructure?

## C. Bulk-Power-System Scope

- [ ] Does any involved transmission facility operate at 69 kV or higher?
- [ ] Are bulk-power-system substations involved?
- [ ] Are utility-scale transformers involved?
- [ ] Are grid-connected inverters involved?
- [ ] Are industrial control systems involved?
- [ ] Are protective relays or intelligent electronic devices involved?
- [ ] Is equipment subject to federal reliability/security requirements?

## D. Equipment Supply Chain

For critical equipment:

- [ ] Manufacturer identified
- [ ] Country of manufacture identified
- [ ] Country of assembly identified
- [ ] Parent company identified
- [ ] Ownership/control identified
- [ ] Critical subcontractors identified
- [ ] Firmware supplier identified
- [ ] Software supplier identified
- [ ] Remote-maintenance provider identified
- [ ] Replacement lead time identified
- [ ] Domestic replacement option identified

## E. Cybersecurity

- [ ] Network architecture documented
- [ ] OT/IT separation documented
- [ ] Remote access documented
- [ ] Vendor remote access documented
- [ ] Authentication controls documented
- [ ] Logging/monitoring documented
- [ ] Firmware update process documented
- [ ] Software update process documented
- [ ] Incident-response plan documented
- [ ] Recovery plan documented
- [ ] Vendor offboarding procedure documented

## F. Resilience

- [ ] Grid outage scenario modeled
- [ ] Transformer failure scenario modeled
- [ ] Substation failure scenario modeled
- [ ] Cyberattack scenario modeled
- [ ] Supply-chain disruption scenario modeled
- [ ] Extreme-weather scenario modeled
- [ ] Backup generation documented
- [ ] Battery storage documented
- [ ] UPS systems documented
- [ ] Fuel supply documented
- [ ] Emergency shutdown documented

## G. Community Impacts

- [ ] Ratepayer impact assessed
- [ ] Transmission infrastructure impact assessed
- [ ] Water demand assessed
- [ ] Cooling technology identified
- [ ] Wastewater/discharge assessed
- [ ] Air emissions assessed
- [ ] Noise assessed
- [ ] Land disturbance assessed
- [ ] Emergency-services demand assessed
- [ ] Local employment quantified
- [ ] Local tax/revenue benefits quantified
- [ ] Community infrastructure investments identified

## H. Governance & Consultation

- [ ] All relevant permits identified
- [ ] Environmental review identified
- [ ] Local zoning identified
- [ ] State review identified
- [ ] Federal review identified
- [ ] Indigenous rights/interests identified
- [ ] Cross-border considerations identified
- [ ] Public information is accessible
- [ ] Technical documents are available
- [ ] Consultation is meaningful and ongoing

## I. Accountability

Ask one question repeatedly:

> **Who benefits, who controls the infrastructure, who pays for it, and who bears the consequences when it fails?**

That question should be answered for:

- power;
- water;
- land;
- cybersecurity;
- emergency response;
- infrastructure upgrades;
- environmental impacts;
- long-term maintenance;
- decommissioning.

---

## Evidence discipline

Classify every answer as:

**CONFIRMED** — supported by a primary document.

**REPORTED** — reported by a credible secondary source but not independently verified.

**INFERRED** — technically plausible but not directly documented.

**UNKNOWN** — information has not been located.

Do not turn UNKNOWN into INFERRED, and do not turn INFERRED into FACT.
