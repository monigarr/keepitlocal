# Akwesasne Implications

> **Framework references:** [Case-study index](00_CASE_STUDY_INDEX.md) · [Sovereignty Model](../../models/sovereignty-model.md) · [Resilience Model](../../models/resilience-model.md) · [Evidence Standard](../../methodology/EVIDENCE_STANDARD.md). Jurisdictional statements distinguish whose position is documented.

## Executive summary

The August 26, 2026 U.S. bulk-power-system emergency does **not** give the Mohawk Council of Akwesasne new jurisdiction over the proposed Massena data-center development.

Its significance is different.

It creates a federal policy context in which the relationship between **AI/data-center growth and critical electrical infrastructure security** is explicitly recognized.

That can strengthen a technically grounded community-information effort focused on understanding infrastructure dependencies and potential impacts.

## 1. Existing Akwesasne concerns

The Mohawk Council of Akwesasne publicly opposed the proposed large-scale data-center development at the former Alcoa East site in Massena and identified concerns including:

- proximity to Akwesasne;
- health, safety, and well-being;
- substantial energy demand;
- substantial water demand;
- potential strain on local power systems and resources;
- impacts on Akwesasne lands and waters;
- need for meaningful consultation and information sharing.

MCA also stated that development should not proceed without proper consultation, meaningful engagement, and community support.

## 2. The new critical-infrastructure lens

The federal order adds another set of questions:

### Electrical resilience

- What infrastructure supplies the proposed load?
- What transmission facilities are involved?
- What substations are involved?
- Which portions are part of the bulk-power system?
- What upgrades are required?

### Equipment

- What transformers are required?
- What generators, UPS systems, batteries, inverters, relays, breakers, and control systems are involved?
- Where are critical components manufactured?
- Who owns or controls the suppliers?

### Digital security

- Which industrial control systems are used?
- Are remote-access capabilities present?
- Who maintains the equipment?
- How are firmware and software updates delivered?
- How is remote access authenticated and monitored?
- What happens if a supplier becomes unavailable?

### Resilience

- What happens during a grid outage?
- What happens during a cyber incident?
- What happens during a transformer or substation failure?
- How long can the facility operate without grid power?
- What backup systems create additional local environmental or safety impacts?

## 3. Cross-border reality

Akwesasne's community reality crosses the U.S.–Canada boundary.

The U.S. Executive Order applies according to U.S. jurisdictional rules. It should not be represented as automatically governing Canadian infrastructure.

Nevertheless, critical infrastructure is interconnected physically and economically.

A responsible Akwesasne assessment should therefore distinguish:

- U.S. federal jurisdiction;
- New York State jurisdiction;
- local municipal jurisdiction;
- Mohawk Council of Akwesasne governance and community interests;
- Canadian federal/provincial jurisdiction where applicable;
- cross-border infrastructure dependencies.

## 4. What this means for Keep It Local

The project can make a useful contribution without pretending to be a regulator.

Its role can be:

> **Translate complex infrastructure dependencies into understandable questions that communities can investigate, verify, and discuss.**

That is consistent with Keep It Local's emphasis on local capability, resilience, knowledge, and reduced dependency.

## 5. A stronger framing

Avoid:

> "The federal government says this data center is dangerous."

Prefer:

> "The federal government has formally recognized that rapid growth in AI and data centers increases dependence on reliable electricity and magnifies the consequences of grid disruption. Communities hosting major AI infrastructure therefore have a legitimate reason to understand the electrical, cybersecurity, supply-chain, and resilience dependencies being introduced into their region."

The second statement is both more accurate and more defensible.
