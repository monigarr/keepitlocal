# Massena AI Infrastructure Research Framework

## Purpose

This document provides a neutral technical framework for researching proposed large-scale data-center development in Massena and its potential relationship to Akwesasne.

It is deliberately structured so that the research can produce either supportive, neutral, or critical findings depending on the evidence.

## Research questions

### 1. What is actually being proposed?

Record:

- developer/entity;
- site;
- parcel(s);
- building count;
- square footage;
- phases;
- intended use;
- estimated electrical load;
- estimated water demand;
- cooling technology;
- expected construction timeline.

### 2. What electrical infrastructure is required?

Map:

`GENERATION → TRANSMISSION → SUBSTATION → TRANSFORMATION → FACILITY → UPS/BATTERY → COMPUTE`

For each element identify:

- owner;
- operator;
- voltage;
- capacity;
- existing/new;
- proposed upgrade;
- permitting authority;
- cost responsibility.

### 3. What is the relationship to the bulk-power system?

Determine whether any project-related transmission facilities, substations, or equipment fall within the federal definition of the bulk-power system.

Do not assume that because a facility consumes large amounts of electricity, every component serving it is automatically subject to the Executive Order.

### 5. What is known about resilience?

Research:

- N-1 assumptions;
- redundancy;
- backup generation;
- battery storage;
- islanding;
- emergency shutdown;
- restoration;
- fuel logistics;
- transformer replacement;
- cyber incident response.

### 6. What is known about community impact?

Research separately:

- electricity;
- water;
- wastewater;
- air;
- noise;
- land;
- traffic;
- emergency services;
- tax/revenue;
- jobs;
- utility/rate impacts.

### 7. What is known about consultation?

Document:

- public meetings;
- planning-board materials;
- environmental review;
- information requests;
- MCA statements;
- municipal responses;
- developer responses;
- unresolved questions.

## Research rule

Do not start with a conclusion.

Start with an infrastructure map.

Then identify:

1. known facts;
2. missing data;
3. dependencies;
4. risks;
5. mitigations;
6. governance questions.

## Current policy context

New York announced a statewide one-year pause on new hyperscale data-center development in July 2026 while developing a regulatory framework addressing ratepayers, energy, water, air quality, environmental impacts, grid infrastructure, and community benefits.

This is relevant context, but New York policy and the federal Executive Order are separate instruments with different jurisdictions and purposes.
