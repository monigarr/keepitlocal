# Keep It Local — Critical Infrastructure Position

> **Framework references:** [Case-study index](00_CASE_STUDY_INDEX.md) · [Sovereign AI principle](../../principles/SOVEREIGN_AI.md) · [Sovereignty Model](../../models/sovereignty-model.md) · [Infrastructure Stack](../../models/infrastructure-stack.md).

## A community-scale infrastructure philosophy

Keep It Local is not an argument against artificial intelligence.

It is an argument for **understanding and reducing unnecessary dependency**.

Modern AI infrastructure can be extremely centralized:

- centralized compute;
- centralized cloud platforms;
- centralized data;
- centralized models;
- centralized power consumption;
- centralized supply chains;
- centralized control.

A resilient community should have alternatives.

## The architecture

A useful model is:

```text
COMMUNITY
   │
   ├── Knowledge
   ├── Language
   ├── Data
   ├── Compute
   ├── Networking
   ├── Energy
   └── Governance
        │
        ▼
LOCAL / SOVEREIGN CAPABILITY
        │
        ▼
REDUCED DEPENDENCY
        │
        ▼
GREATER RESILIENCE
```

## Sovereign AI

Sovereign AI does not have to mean building a hyperscale data center.

For many communities, it can mean:

- local models;
- local inference;
- local storage;
- local backups;
- local networking;
- local knowledge bases;
- controlled access;
- community governance;
- appropriate offline capability.

This can reduce dependence on external cloud infrastructure for selected workloads.

## Indigenous knowledge and language

Community-controlled AI is particularly important where data contains:

- language knowledge;
- cultural knowledge;
- oral histories;
- community records;
- locally generated datasets;
- sensitive intellectual property.

The same architectural principle that motivates Kanien'kéha Language Intelligence can be applied to broader community digital infrastructure:

> **The people who generate and steward the knowledge should have meaningful control over the infrastructure through which that knowledge is stored, processed, and used.**

## Critical infrastructure

The August 2026 federal action provides a national-scale example of this principle.

The federal government is concerned about dependency on foreign-produced critical electrical equipment, associated software, firmware, remote access, maintenance, and supply chains.

Keep It Local asks a related community-scale question:

> **How much of a community's essential digital capability should depend on infrastructure it does not control?**

## The objective

The objective is not isolation.

It is **resilience**.

A resilient community can still use:

- cloud services;
- external AI;
- public networks;
- regional utilities;
- commercial platforms.

But it should know:

- what depends on them;
- what happens when they fail;
- what alternatives exist;
- what data leaves the community;
- who controls the infrastructure;
- what recovery looks like.

## Design principle

> **Use the cloud when it makes sense. Keep what is essential close to home. Know the difference.**

That is the Keep It Local principle.
